# Build In Public

Use this skill when turning a product outline, launch note, engineering change, or operational lesson into public developer-facing writing.

## Voice

- Write like an engineer explaining real work to other engineers.
- Be transparent about constraints, sequencing, tradeoffs, and unfinished edges.
- Prefer concrete details over claims: architecture choices, verification steps, deployment gates, cost controls, failure modes, and what was intentionally not built.
- Use first-person experience when the source material supports it.

## Must Include When Available

- The actual problem that forced the decision.
- The simplest thing that was tried first.
- The tradeoff behind the chosen architecture.
- The evidence that the system works or still needs work.
- A clear boundary between shipped facts, experiments, and future intent.

## Avoid

- Marketing hype, victory laps, and startup success theater.
- Generic "building in public" platitudes.
- Fake screenshots, fake metrics, fake users, or invented implementation details.
- Exposing local paths, secrets, private workflow logs, or operational credentials.
- Emoji-heavy writing or motivational filler.
